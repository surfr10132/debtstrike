# Credit Control LLC: How to Respond and Protect Yourself

A letter from Credit Control LLC usually means a bank or lender has handed off your account to a third-party collection agency. Here's the important part: **you have 30 days from their first contact to demand proof that the debt is real and that they have the right to collect it.**

That right comes from **FDCPA § 1692g** — and most people who get letters from collectors never use it.

This page breaks down who Credit Control LLC is, what they're legally allowed to do, and how to respond in a way that actually protects you.

---

## Who Is Credit Control LLC?

Credit Control LLC is a third-party debt collection agency. They don't own your debt — they collect on behalf of the original creditor (usually a bank, credit card company, or lender). They get paid when they collect from you.

This distinction matters because:

1. **They must follow FDCPA rules.** As a third-party collector, every interaction they have with you is governed by federal law. Original creditors have more leeway — third-party collectors do not.

2. **They may not have complete records.** When a creditor hands off an account to a collection agency, the full documentation doesn't always follow. Account statements, original agreements, and payment histories can be missing or incomplete.

---

## What Credit Control LLC Can and Cannot Do

Under the **Fair Debt Collection Practices Act (FDCPA)**, Credit Control is bound by strict rules about how they can contact you and what they can say.

**They cannot:**
- Call you before 8 a.m. or after 9 p.m. (§ 1692c)
- Call your workplace if you tell them your employer doesn't allow it (§ 1692c)
- Contact you at all after you send a written cease and desist (§ 1692c)
- Use abusive, threatening, or obscene language (§ 1692d)
- Lie about the amount you owe or who they are (§ 1692e)
- Threaten to sue if they don't actually plan to (§ 1692e)

Every violation is worth **up to $1,000 in statutory damages** under § 1692k — plus your attorney's fees if you take legal action.

**They can:**
- Contact you by mail, phone, or text (until you restrict it)
- Report the debt to credit bureaus
- File a lawsuit within the statute of limitations

---

## Your 30-Day Validation Window

Under **FDCPA § 1692g**, within **5 days of first contacting you**, Credit Control must send a written notice that includes the amount of the debt, the name of the creditor, and a statement that you have 30 days to dispute it.

If you dispute the debt **in writing** within those 30 days, Credit Control must stop all collection until they send you verification. No calls, no letters, nothing — until they prove the debt is valid and that they have the right to collect it.

**The 30 days starts from their first contact.** Don't wait.

---

## Two Letters That Shift the Power

### Letter 1: Debt Validation Letter

This letter invokes your right under § 1692g to demand proof of the debt. It disputes the balance, demands documentation of the original agreement, and requires Credit Control to show the chain of assignment from the original creditor to them.

Once they receive this letter, collection activity must stop until they verify everything. If they keep calling — that's an FDCPA violation.

### Letter 2: Cease and Desist Letter

Under **FDCPA § 1692c(c)**, you can tell Credit Control to stop all contact permanently. After receiving your letter, they can only:
- Send one final letter confirming they'll stop
- Notify you of a specific legal action they plan to take

Any other contact after that is a federal violation.

## How to Send It

Send your letter by **USPS Certified Mail with Return Receipt Requested**.

- Certified Mail proves the date they received it
- The Return Receipt (green card) comes back with their signature
- This is your evidence if they violate the law after receiving your letter

Keep everything — the envelope, the receipt, the tracking number. If they call you after the letter arrives and you have that proof, you have a case.

## Frequently Asked Questions

### Is Credit Control LLC a real company?

Yes. Credit Control LLC is a third-party collection agency that collects debts on behalf of banks and lenders. They are not your original creditor — they were hired or assigned to collect. Because they are a third-party collector, they are subject to every rule in the FDCPA.

### Can I ignore their letters?

You can, but ignoring a collector doesn't make the debt go away. If the debt is within the statute of limitations, they can escalate to a lawsuit. The smarter move is to respond in writing — demand proof under § 1692g and tell them to stop contacting you under § 1692c(c).

### What if Credit Control LLC is calling me about a debt I already paid?

If you already paid the original creditor, Credit Control has no right to collect. Send a validation letter demanding proof of the outstanding balance. If they continue to collect on a paid debt, that's a violation of § 1692e.

### Will responding restart the statute of limitations?

No. Sending a written dispute or validation letter does not restart the statute of limitations. Only making a payment or a written promise to pay can restart it in most states. Exercising your federal rights is not an acknowledgment of the debt.

### What if my 30 days have already passed?

You can still send a cease and desist at any time under § 1692c(c). The 30-day validation window has a deadline, but your right to tell them to stop contacting you never expires.
