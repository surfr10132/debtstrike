# Solaris Assets LLC: How to Respond to Their Collection Letter

If Solaris Assets LLC — or their affiliated entity SunStrong Management — sent you a letter about a debt, here's what matters most: **you have 30 days from their first contact to demand proof that the debt is real, that the amount is correct, and that they have the legal right to collect it.**

That right exists under **FDCPA § 1692g**, and it's designed for situations exactly like this — where a company you've never heard of claims you owe them money.

---

## Who Is Solaris Assets LLC?

Solaris Assets is a debt buyer. They purchase portfolios of defaulted consumer debts from banks, credit card companies, and other lenders — typically for pennies on the dollar. Then they attempt to collect the full balance from you.

They sometimes operate through affiliated companies like **SunStrong Management**, which may handle the actual collection calls and letters on debts that Solaris owns.

Here's why this matters:

1. **They bought your debt secondhand.** You never entered into any agreement with Solaris Assets. Your original creditor sold the account. Under federal law, Solaris has to prove they actually own the debt and that the amount is accurate before they can keep pursuing you.

2. **Documentation gaps are common.** When debts are sold in bulk, the original signed agreements, account statements, and payment histories don't always transfer completely. Solaris may be working from a spreadsheet entry — not a full paper trail.

---

## What They Can and Cannot Do

As a third-party debt collector, Solaris Assets (and SunStrong Management) must follow every rule in the **Fair Debt Collection Practices Act (FDCPA)**.

**They cannot:**
- Call you before 8 a.m. or after 9 p.m. (§ 1692c)
- Call your workplace if you tell them your employer prohibits it (§ 1692c)
- Contact you after you send a written cease and desist (§ 1692c)
- Use threats, abuse, or profanity (§ 1692d)
- Misrepresent the amount you owe or their identity (§ 1692e)
- Threaten legal action they have no intention of pursuing (§ 1692e)

Each violation is worth **up to $1,000 in statutory damages** under § 1692k — and if you take legal action, they pay your attorney's fees too.

**They can:**
- Contact you by mail, phone, or text (until you restrict it in writing)
- Report the debt to credit bureaus
- File a lawsuit within the statute of limitations

---

## Your 30-Day Window to Demand Proof

Under **FDCPA § 1692g**, within **5 days of their first contact**, Solaris Assets must send you a notice that includes the debt amount, the name of the original creditor, and a statement that you can dispute within 30 days.

If you dispute the debt **in writing** within that 30-day window, they must stop all collection activity until they send you verification — the original agreement, account statements, and proof of the chain of ownership from the original creditor to Solaris.

**The 30 days starts from their first contact — not the day you open the letter.** Respond quickly.

## Two Letters That Put You in Control

### Letter 1: Debt Validation Letter

This letter invokes your § 1692g rights. It demands that Solaris produce the original signed agreement, a complete accounting of the balance claimed, and documentation showing how the debt was transferred from the original creditor to Solaris Assets.

Once they receive it, all collection must stop until they verify. If they can't produce the documentation — and with purchased debt, they often can't — they have to walk away.

### Letter 2: Cease and Desist Letter

Under **FDCPA § 1692c(c)**, you can order any debt collector to stop all contact with you. After receiving your letter, their only options are to send one final confirmation letter or notify you of a specific legal action they intend to take.

Any other contact after that is a federal violation.

## How to Send It

Send by **USPS Certified Mail with Return Receipt Requested**.

- Certified Mail creates a delivery record that proves when they received your letter
- The Return Receipt (green card) comes back with a signature
- This paper trail becomes your evidence if they violate the law after receiving your dispute

Keep the receipt, the tracking number, and a copy of the letter. If they call or write you after the letter arrives, document everything.

## Frequently Asked Questions

### Who is Solaris Assets LLC?

Solaris Assets is a debt buyer that purchases portfolios of defaulted consumer debts from original creditors. They sometimes operate through affiliated entities like SunStrong Management. As a third-party collector, they must follow every FDCPA rule.

### What is SunStrong Management?

SunStrong Management is affiliated with Solaris Assets and may handle collection activity on debts Solaris owns. Your FDCPA rights apply regardless of which entity contacts you.

### Can Solaris Assets prove they own my debt?

Maybe not. Debt buyers purchase accounts in bulk, and documentation often doesn't transfer completely. Demanding validation forces them to produce the paperwork — or stop collecting.

### Does sending a letter restart the statute of limitations?

No. A validation demand or dispute letter does not restart the statute of limitations. Only making a payment or entering into a new agreement to pay can restart it. Exercising your federal rights cannot be used against you.

### What if they already reported this to my credit?

Dispute the tradeline with the credit bureaus and demand Solaris validate the debt. If they can't validate, they're required to remove the tradeline. Send the validation letter first.
